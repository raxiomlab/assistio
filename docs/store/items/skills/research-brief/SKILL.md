# Deep research brief

Use this skill whenever the user asks for research, a comparison, a market scan,
or evidence to support a decision.

## Method

1. **Restate the question** in one sentence, including the decision it supports and
   any constraints (date range, geography, audience).
2. **Plan 3-6 searches** that attack the question from different angles: primary
   data, official documentation, independent analysis, and dissenting views.
3. **Open every source** you intend to cite. Never cite a search-result snippet.
4. **Extract evidence** into a table: claim, source URL, publication date, sample
   or scope, and strength (strong / moderate / weak).
5. **Look for contradictions.** If credible sources disagree, say so and explain
   what evidence would settle it.
6. **Write the brief** using the output contract below.

## Output contract

```
# <Question>

## Answer
3-5 sentences. No preamble. State confidence at the end.

## Evidence
| Claim | Source | Date | Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Confidence
- Finding — high/medium/low — why

## Contradictions
- What disagrees, and what would resolve it

## Gaps and next checks
- What could not be verified
```

## Rules

- A source is a URL you actually opened and read.
- Keep the original unit, period and definition for every number.
- Mark unsourced statements as **unverified**.
- Label vendor pages as vendor claims; do not present them as independent evidence.
- Prefer primary sources; use commentary only to explain or contextualise.
- If the question cannot be answered with available sources, say so and return the
  best partial answer with explicit gaps.
- Do not invent citations, dates, sample sizes or quotes.

## Tone

Direct, specific and calm. No hype. If the evidence is thin, the brief should say
it is thin.
