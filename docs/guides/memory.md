# Memory & knowledge

Most assistants forget everything between conversations. AssistIO **remembers** — in a
structured, searchable way that stays on your machine.

## Three kinds of memory

- **Semantic** — stable facts and preferences. *("The user prefers a dense UI.")*
- **Episodic** — things that happened. Decisions and events, with context.
- **Procedural** — how to do something. Reusable procedures and workflows.

Memory is **organized automatically**: what a conversation teaches is extracted and
filed for you, with a scope and a lifetime attached.

## Scopes

Every fact is stored at the right level:

- **Per conversation** — only relevant to the current chat.
- **Per user** — follows you across conversations.
- **Global** — applies everywhere.

## Retention

Not everything is equally hot. Memories carry **hot, warm and cold** retention tiers
with automatic expiry, so recent, frequently-used facts stay close at hand while older
ones settle into long-term storage without cluttering the active context.

## Knowledge graph

Facts are not stored as a flat list. A **knowledge graph** links entities and their
relationships — people, projects, tools and decisions — so the agent can reason about
how things connect, not just recall isolated facts.

## Knowledge bases

You can attach **local folders** as a knowledge base. AssistIO indexes them and keeps
them up to date automatically, so the agent can ground its answers in your own
documents.

> Memory and knowledge live on your machine. They are never sent to a provider except
> as part of the conversation you choose to have — and sensitive content is redacted
> first. See [Privacy & security](privacy-and-security.md).
